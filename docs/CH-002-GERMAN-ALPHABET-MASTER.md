## Stage 02 — English Connection

German and English both use the Latin alphabet. Written shapes are familiar, but German letter names and sounds can be different.

| German | English | Note |
|---|---|---|
| das Alphabet | the alphabet | Related words |
| der Name | the name | Capitalized German noun |
| das Wort | the word | German W often has a v-like sound |
| der Buchstabe | the letter | German noun |

## Stage 06 — Special German Characters

Ä, Ö and Ü are umlaut letters. ß is called Eszett or sharp S. In systems without these characters: ä→ae, ö→oe, ü→ue, ß→ss (fallback spelling only).

| German | English | Pronunciation note |
|---|---|---|
| Äpfel | apples | Ä: vowel sound varies by word |
| schön | beautiful / nice | Ö requires rounded lips |
| fünf | five | Ü requires rounded lips |
| Straße | street | ß represents an s sound |

## Stage 07 — Grammar Made Clear

German nouns begin with capital letters: der Name, das Wort, der Buchstabe, das Alphabet. Sentences start with a capital too.

Ich heiße Anna. / Mein Name ist Anna. Both can mean “My name is Anna.”


**Practice A10 (Multiple choice)** — Which is correctly capitalized?
A: der name; B: der Name; C: der NAME
Correct: B
Feedback: German nouns are capitalized.

## Stage 13 — Common Mistake Alert

Do not call German J “jay” or Z “zee.” Umlauts change sounds; ß is not the capital letter B. Use Google Listener to hear difficult sounds.

Pronunciation hints are approximate. A German letter in a word can sound different from its standalone name.
